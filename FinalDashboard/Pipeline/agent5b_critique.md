## Critique rubric (apply to every answer)

### For each recommendation, check:

  □ GEOGRAPHY SPECIFIC: Does it name a specific area of Phoenix/Maricopa?
    ("south Phoenix" = too vague)
    ("the 14 High-High cluster tracts between S Central Ave and S 27th Ave,
     approximately 3 miles south of downtown Phoenix" = specific enough)

  □ STATISTIC CITED: Does it reference a specific number from the dashboard?
    ("high hardship" = fail)
    ("hardship index of 1.42, ranking in the top 5% nationally" = pass)

  □ ENTITY NAMED: Does it name a specific responsible organization?
    ("the city should" = fail)
    ("Maricopa County Community Services Department + City of Phoenix
     Neighborhood Services Department" = pass)

  □ INTERVENTION CONCRETE: Is the proposed action specific?
    ("invest in these communities" = fail)
    ("expand the existing First Place AZ transitional housing program to
     3 additional tracts, targeting households earning under 30% AMI" = pass)

  □ CAUSAL REASONING: Does it connect the spatial pattern to a mechanism?
    Does it avoid claiming census data shows what it cannot show (causality)?
    Does it acknowledge the displacement paradox where relevant?

  □ LENGTH: Is it 3-4 substantive sentences (not bullet points)?

### For the executive summary, check:
  □ States the county name
  □ Gives a specific hardship rank or comparison to national mean
  □ Names the spatial clustering strength (Moran's I value)
  □ Identifies the most urgent finding with a number
  □ States the top recommendation in one sentence
  □ Is exactly 3-4 sentences (no more)

### For the finding paragraphs, check:
  □ Each cites at least 2 specific numbers
  □ Each interprets the visualization (not just describes it)
  □ Finding 2 specifically addresses the displacement paradox
  □ Finding 3 compares trajectory types quantitatively
